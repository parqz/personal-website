I'm a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at Georgia State University, advised by
Dr. Rajshekhar Sunderraman and Dr. Yi Ding (University of Tennessee, Knoxville), and a graduate
research assistant at GSU's CHAI Center. I work on **efficient and trustworthy AI**: systems that
are not only capable, but also computationally efficient, reliable, privacy-preserving and practical
to deploy.

A common thread runs through my work: improving AI under real-world constraints, whether that means
limited compute, noisy supervision, privacy requirements or undesirable model behavior. My recent
work tests what actually makes large models cheaper to adapt and run: a controlled study of rank
allocation in LoRA (under review), and an analysis of how visual-token pruning in vision-language
models is evaluated. I also study how reliable LLM systems are, including authority bias in
LLM-based search engines and learning from noisy, disagreeing human annotators (two EMNLP 2026
papers). Earlier, I worked on making encrypted inference with homomorphic encryption faster and
lighter on memory (IEEE TPS, ACNS and IEEE S&P).

More detail on each project is on the [Research]({{ '/research/' | relative_url }}) page.
